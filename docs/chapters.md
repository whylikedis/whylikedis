# The chapters, and what each must answer

Every chapter is a set of fact cards. A chapter is written as questions to test, not as
conclusions to prove. If the evidence does not support what we expected, the card says so, and the
question moves to `docs/hypotheses.md` with what was found.

## Where we stand

We are Israelis who love our country. We stand with Israel, and we criticise injustice wherever
it happens, including in Israel and the territories it controls. A card that leaves out a
documented wrong because it is inconvenient fails review. A fact that cuts against the answer goes
on the card.

**On the word "genocide".** We do not say that Israel has committed genocide in Gaza, because we
do not believe the legal definition is met. Our cards say what the Genocide Convention requires,
show what recognised genocides looked like, and set out what Israel could do and what it did. They
also carry, accurately, the findings of the bodies that have concluded otherwise, and the facts
those findings rest on. A reader should be able to disagree with us using only our own cards.

When an answer gives our conclusion and not only facts, it says "our reading".

## Rules for chapters 9 to 13

These chapters are where a fact base most easily turns into an argument. So:

- Chapter 9 never says that because others did it, it was right. It says what happened, and what
  the law was.
- Chapter 10 reports amounts, owners and dates from public filings. It does not infer motives or
  effects that no source documents.
- Chapter 11 describes people's stated reasons in their own words. It does not diagnose them.
- Chapter 12 lists refusals and wrongs by each side. It does not turn a list into a verdict.
- Chapter 13 compares countries feature by feature, with a source for each cell. It does not use
  one word, such as "racist" or "democratic", to stand for a whole country.
- About any living politician, cards carry the public record only: court documents, votes, dates,
  polls. What we think of a leader's motives is opinion, and it is labelled "our reading" or left
  out.
- Numbers we compute ourselves, such as from election results, come with the script and the
  official file it read, so anyone can run it again.
